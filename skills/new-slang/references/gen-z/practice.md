# Practice — the curriculum

For someone starting from zero. The ordering matters more than the content: **comprehension before production, always.** Every embarrassing outcome comes from someone producing before they could reliably parse.

The goal is not to sound 22. It's to **understand everything and deploy a little, perfectly.** That's what a fluent second-language speaker sounds like, and it's a far better outcome than the alternative.

---

## Week 1 — Fix the tells (zero vocabulary)

**Highest return in the whole curriculum, and not one slang word is involved.** These are mechanical, permanent, and they're currently costing you.

- [ ] Stop using 😂. Replace with 💀 or 😭. This is the single loudest signal you emit.
- [ ] Stop replying with 👍. It reads as dismissal. Use "got it", "nice", ✅, or 🙌.
- [ ] Drop terminal periods in casual messages. `ok.` → `ok`
- [ ] Drop capitalization in casual messages. `Sounds good` → `sounds good`
- [ ] Stop using "..." — it reads as judgment.
- [ ] Read `references/decoding.md` §1–3 twice. That's the tone system.

**Drill:** rewrite your last 10 sent Slack/text messages under these rules. Run `/slang check` on three of them.

**Success condition:** you can send a casual message that doesn't look like an email.

## Week 2 — Comprehension only

Consume, don't produce. You are building a receptive vocabulary and, more importantly, an ear for **dosage** — how sparingly fluent people actually deploy this.

**Where to actually listen** (this is the part guides leave out):
- **TikTok / Reels comment sections.** Not the videos — the *comments*. This is the densest natural corpus in existence, and you see terms used in context by real people rather than performed by creators.
- **Twitch chat**, any large stream. Fast, native, heavy on gaming-origin terms.
- **Reddit:** r/GenZ, r/OutOfTheLoop (explicitly for asking what things mean — built for exactly your situation).
- **Group chats you're already in** with anyone under 30. Reread with new eyes.
- **Younger colleagues' Slack messages.** Free, relevant, and directly applicable.

**Daily drill (10 min):** collect 3 phrases you didn't fully understand. Run `/slang wtf` on each. Note the *register flag*, not just the meaning.

**Weekly:** `/slang drill` twice, decode-only mode.

**Success condition:** you can read a comment section and follow it without stopping. You do not say anything yet.

## Week 3 — Patterns, not words

Now produce — but learn **constructions**, not vocabulary. Ten patterns generate more fluent output than a hundred memorized words, and they don't expire.

Take one pattern per day from `usage-guide.md` §2 and generate five real sentences about your actual life:

| Day | Pattern | Your five sentences about... |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `it's giving ___` | things at work today |
| 2 | `not me ___ing` | things you actually did |
| 3 | `the way ___` | things that annoyed you |
| 4 | `___ era` | phases you've been in |
| 5 | `I fear ___` | bad news you had to deliver |
| 6 | `no because ___` | things you feel strongly about |
| 7 | `___-coded` / `___ behavior` | people and things around you |

Run `/slang check` on your five each day. Take the grade seriously — a soft grade now is an embarrassment later.

**Success condition:** you can produce a natural sentence using a pattern without a word from the glossary in it. That's the real milestone — patterns carry fluency, vocabulary just decorates it.

## Week 4 — Deploy, sparingly

Live fire, minimum dose.

- [ ] One slang term, once a day, to one person who'd get it.
- [ ] **Praise only.** "that ate", "you understood the assignment", "big W". Zero downside.
- [ ] Deadpan delivery. No exclamation points, no emoji pile.
- [ ] If nobody reacts, you did it right. **Silence is success.** A reaction means it stuck out, which means you overdid it.
- [ ] Never explain it afterward. Ever.

**Success condition:** a slang term passes unremarked in a real conversation.

## Ongoing — maintenance

- **Monthly:** `/slang drill`, full mode.
- **Quarterly:** re-audit for dead terms. Anything you learned 6+ months ago is suspect.
- **Always:** if you see it in an ad, retire it that day.
- **Never chase.** Chasing the newest terms is the failure mode, not the goal. Precision reads as fluent; recency reads as desperate.

## Drill banks

### A. Decode (receptive)

Translate to plain English, **including tone**:

1. `ok.` (from a 24-year-old)
2. "no because why did they schedule it at 4pm 😭"
3. "the way this doc has no owner"
4. "I fear the client already saw it"
5. "that's crazy" (as the reply ending a thread)
6. "lowkey the first version ate"
7. "he's yapping again but he's kind of standing on business"
8. "not me opening the PR and immediately closing it 💀"
9. "the vibes are off but I can't explain why"
10. "it's giving Q4 panic"

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Cold, annoyed. Not neutral. The period is the whole message.
2. Exasperated but joking — 😭 is laughing. Not distress.
3. Incredulous — "unbelievably, this doc has no owner." No main clause needed.
4. Softened bad news: the client already saw it. Mock-formal, apologetic.
5. **Polite disengagement.** They're done talking, not agreeing. Common misread.
6. "Honestly, the first version was better." Mild, hedged, sincere.
7. He's talking a lot, but he's holding a serious position and following through. Net positive.
8. Self-deprecating: opened the PR, saw the state of it, noped out. Joking.
9. Genuine unease, no specific cause identified. Take it seriously — it's often a real signal delivered casually.
10. This has the character of end-of-quarter panic. Diagnostic, mildly mocking.
</details>

### B. Produce (expressive)

Say each in fluent slang, **one term max**:

1. The presentation was outstanding.
2. This project is in serious trouble.
3. The redesign is mediocre.
4. I'm going through an intense work phase.
5. That was wildly inappropriate.
6. I have bad news about the deadline.
7. He's praising the CEO embarrassingly.
8. Things have finally turned around.

<details><summary>Sample answers</summary>

1. "that presentation ate"
2. "we're cooked"
3. "the redesign is mid"
4. "I'm in my grind era" / "locked in rn"
5. "that was out of pocket"
6. "I fear the deadline moved"
7. "he's glazing so hard"
8. "we're so back"

Each is **one** term in an otherwise ordinary sentence. If your answer had two, that's the error.

## Self-test — are you fluent?

You're there when you can:

- [ ] Read a comment section without stopping
- [ ] Tell whether a message is warm or cold from **punctuation alone**, ignoring words
- [ ] Generate a sentence from a pattern with no glossary word in it
- [ ] Name why a given term is safe or unsafe **for you specifically**
- [ ] Identify a dead term without looking it up
- [ ] Deploy one term and have nobody notice
- [ ] Resist deploying when the register is wrong — **the real skill**

The last one is the one that separates fluency from tourism. Knowing when not to is the whole game.
